## Context
- Measured by (KPIs): no formal KPIs; success means things get done without having to nag, the split feels fair, and the organizer carries less of the mental load [E0: industry assumption]; the planning, remembering, and monitoring part of household work is unevenly split, reported at 71 % for mothers [E1: https://remindher.app/best-task-sharing-apps-couples/]
- Current tools: WhatsApp/Signal groups, a paper cleaning schedule on the fridge, shared notes, Apple Reminders shared lists within iCloud [E1: https://support.apple.com/en-us/105124]; specialized apps such as Flatastic for flat shares [E1: https://www.flatastic-app.com/en/], Cozi for families [E1: https://www.cozi.com/to-do-lists/], Spond for clubs [E1: https://www.spond.com/de/]; in clubs also Excel and "hoping for the best" [E1: https://www.spond.com/news-and-blog/volunteer-management-system/]
- Decision power: informal only; can propose a tool but cannot force anyone to use it [E0: industry assumption]; in clubs, participation in WhatsApp groups must be voluntary under GDPR, so the organizer cannot mandate a channel [E1: https://www.vibss.de/vereinsmanagement/marketing/social-media/whatsapp/whatsapp-rechtslage-mit-einfuehrung-der-eu-dsgvo]
- Time budget and technical affinity: organizes in the evening, on weekends, or in gaps, almost always on the phone; expects zero setup [E0: industry assumption]; the organizer is usually the most tech-affine person in the group, while others (children, grandparents, older club members) vary widely [E0: industry assumption]
- Typical workday: organizing is a side job next to paid work or studies; triggers are an empty fridge, a dirty kitchen, an upcoming event, or a new flatmate [E0: industry assumption]

## Typical industry flows
Recurring chores in a flat share or family [E0: industry assumption]:
1. At a flat meeting or at the kitchen table, the group agrees on chores and a rotation; the organizer writes it down. [E0: industry assumption]
2. The plan hangs on the fridge or sits in a chat message or chore app. [E0: industry assumption]
3. During the week the organizer notices undone tasks and reminds the person in chat or face to face. [E0: industry assumption]
4. Rigid schedules need someone to enforce them; that person becomes the unwanted "cleaning police", and the plan fades. [E1: https://liastoeffler.de/blog/3-gr%C3%BCnde-warum-putzen-in-der-wg-nicht-funktioniert]
5. When people spend unequal time at home, the equal split feels unfair and triggers a discussion. [E1: https://liastoeffler.de/blog/3-gr%C3%BCnde-warum-putzen-in-der-wg-nicht-funktioniert]
6. Some groups switch to an app with points and reminders so "everyone sees who has to do what and how much each person has done". [E1: https://www.flatastic-app.com/en/]

Shopping [E0: industry assumption]:
1. Whoever notices something is missing posts it in the group chat or adds it to a shared list. [E0: industry assumption]
2. The person at the store scrolls the chat or opens the list and ticks items off. [E1: https://www.cozi.com/to-do-lists/]
3. Items get bought twice or not at all when the chat and the list disagree. [E0: industry assumption]

Club event with helpers [E0: industry assumption]:
1. The organizer lists helper tasks (driver, stand shift, cake) in a chat post, a spreadsheet, or a club app. [E1: https://www.spond.com/de/]
2. Some tasks are assigned to specific people, others are open for volunteers to pick and confirm. [E1: http://www.taekwondo-bautzen.de/uploads/file/Dokumente/Anleitung-App-SPOND.pdf]
3. Replies arrive in the chat; the organizer copies them into the list by hand. [E0: industry assumption]
4. Shortly before the event, the organizer chases open slots personally. [E0: industry assumption]

## Pain points (sorted by severity)
- Being the one who has to remind and enforce; turns them into the "cleaning police" and strains relationships [E1: https://liastoeffler.de/blog/3-gr%C3%BCnde-warum-putzen-in-der-wg-nicht-funktioniert]
- Carrying the invisible mental load of anticipating and planning, while others only execute [E1: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6749406018]
- Disputes about fairness when effort and presence differ [E1: https://liastoeffler.de/blog/3-gr%C3%BCnde-warum-putzen-in-der-wg-nicht-funktioniert]
- Coordination through WhatsApp groups and spreadsheets is time-consuming and unreliable [E1: https://www.spond.com/news-and-blog/volunteer-management-system/]
- Getting everyone onto the same tool: iCloud lists exclude Android users, other apps need an account or install [E0: industry assumption]
- In clubs, WhatsApp raises GDPR concerns (phone numbers visible, consent, youth sections) [E1: https://klublink.de/whatsapp-im-verein-dsgvo.php]
- Free versions of family apps come with ads or new limits (Cozi free calendar limited to 30 days) [E1: https://www.usecalendara.com/blog/cozi-review-2026]

## Critical assumptions
