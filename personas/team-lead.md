Buying function: Buyer, Administrator | Segment: Agencies, freelancer collectives, small businesses (office, trades); teams of 2–20, DACH first

## Context
- Measured by (KPIs): work delivered on time and within scope [E1: https://www.naukri.com/career-advice/team-leader-roles-and-responsibilities]; in agencies also team utilization and client satisfaction [E0: industry assumption]; in trades, jobs finished without call-backs and no idle time on site [E0: industry assumption]
- Current tools: plans "in the boss's head", on a whiteboard, or through countless WhatsApp messages [E1: https://mein-handwerker-app.de/einsatzplanung-handwerk-tipps/]; 91 % of German craft businesses use messengers for internal communication [E1: https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Handwerk-wird-digitaler]; agencies and office teams additionally use free tiers of Trello, Asana, Todoist, Microsoft To Do, or Notion, plus spreadsheets [E0: industry assumption]
- Decision power: can introduce a free tool for their own team without formal approval; anything paid or anything touching client data needs the owner's OK [E0: industry assumption]
- Time budget and technical affinity: spends most of the day on delivery work, coordination is squeezed in between; little patience for tool setup (minutes, not hours) [E0: industry assumption]; high technical affinity in agencies, mixed in trades, where 76 % of businesses say employees need more digital competence [E1: https://www.bitkom.org/sites/main/files/2026-01/bitkom-studienbericht-handwerk.pdf]
- Typical workday: does own client or production work, answers ad-hoc requests from clients, colleagues, or the owner, and redistributes work when someone is sick or a job overruns [E0: industry assumption]; takes over the weekly planning and delegates tasks, responsibilities, and working hours [E1: https://www.jobverde.de/karriereinfo/teamleitung-der-job-als-teamleiterin]

## Jobs to be done
- Plan the team's week: break incoming work into tasks and give each task to one person | Frequency: weekly [E1: https://www.jobverde.de/karriereinfo/teamleitung-der-job-als-teamleiterin]
- Check what is due today and who is behind | Frequency: daily [E0: industry assumption]
- Delegate ad-hoc tasks that come in by phone, email, or chat | Frequency: daily [E0: industry assumption]
- Monitor individual and team progress without holding a meeting | Frequency: daily [E1: https://resources.workable.com/team-leader-job-description]
- Rebalance work when someone is absent or a job overruns | Frequency: weekly [E0: industry assumption]
- Bring a freelancer or temporary helper into the team's list for a project and remove them afterwards | Frequency: monthly [E0: industry assumption]
- Report status to the owner or client | Frequency: weekly [E0: industry assumption]

## Typical industry flows
Planning the week (agency / office) [E0: industry assumption]:
1. Collects open work from client emails, the owner, last week's leftovers, and chat messages. [E0: industry assumption]
2. Writes a list in a notebook, spreadsheet, or project tool, often on Monday morning or Friday afternoon. [E0: industry assumption]
3. Leaves about 20 % of capacity unplanned for the unexpected. [E1: https://www.teamleader.eu/de/blog/ressourcenplanung-vorlage]
4. Assigns tasks in a short team meeting or by posting the list into the team chat. [E0: industry assumption]
5. During the week, asks in chat or in person "Is X done?"; answers arrive in the chat and are not reflected in the list. [E0: industry assumption]
6. At the end of the week, rebuilds the list from memory and chat history. [E0: industry assumption]

Planning the week (trades) [E0: industry assumption]:
1. Plans jobs per person and day on a whiteboard, on paper, or in the owner's head. [E1: https://mein-handwerker-app.de/einsatzplanung-handwerk-tipps/]
2. Sends the plan and changes to workers via WhatsApp messages or calls. [E1: https://mein-handwerker-app.de/einsatzplanung-handwerk-tipps/]
3. Workers report completion or problems by call or chat. [E0: industry assumption]
4. From about 5–6 people, whiteboard/Excel planning becomes increasingly error-prone, which is when trades businesses look for a digital tool. [E1: https://mein-handwerker-app.de/einsatzplanung-handwerk-tipps/]

Bringing someone into a shared tool today [E0: industry assumption]:
1. Invites by email in the chosen tool. [E1: https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/collaborate-in-todoist-WOpFVjup7]
2. The invitee must have or create a matching account; in Microsoft To Do both need Exchange Online-backed accounts and sharing across organizations is not supported. [E1: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/todo/create-and-share-lists]
3. Free tiers cap collaborators (Trello 10 per workspace, Todoist 5 per personal project, Asana Personal 2 users), so growing teams hit a paywall. [E1: https://support.atlassian.com/trello/docs/workspace-user-limit/] [E1: https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/usage-limits-in-todoist-e5rcSY] [E1: https://costbench.com/software/project-management/asana/free-plan/]

## Pain points (sorted by severity)
- Tasks handed out in chat disappear in long threads; responsibility is implied rather than assigned, and nobody sees what is pending or overdue [E1: https://flowhubr.com/blog/project-management/why-using-whatsapp-to-manage-internal-tasks-fails-for-smes/]
- Has to chase people for status because completion is reported in chat or not at all [E0: industry assumption]
- Heavy project tools are not adopted by the team; learning curve is the top complaint about Asana on G2 [E1: https://www.g2.com/products/asana/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons]
- Free tiers of known tools cap team size, and the lead must justify a paid plan to the owner [E1: https://support.atlassian.com/trello/docs/workspace-user-limit/] [E1: https://www.capterra.com/p/211559/Trello/reviews/]
- Freelancers and external helpers cannot join because of account or organization restrictions [E1: https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/todo/create-and-share-lists]
- Assigning a task in Microsoft To Do requires setting up a shared list and waiting for the other person to join; reminders can't be set for others [E1: https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/to-do/to-do-task-assignment-feature-is-inconvenient--unable-to-set-reminders-for-tasks/4028520]
- Weekly plan gets outdated within a day, and updating it is extra work nobody sees [E0: industry assumption]

## Relationships to other roles
- Task contributor: the lead assigns work and depends on contributors marking tasks done; contributors can veto the tool by simply not using it [E0: industry assumption]
- Business owner (candidate): approves paid tools and anything involving client data; wants an overview without doing tasks; in very small firms the owner is often the team lead [E0: industry assumption]
- Client stakeholder (candidate): asks for status, which the lead currently compiles by hand [E0: industry assumption]
- Field worker (candidate): in trades, receives the plan on the phone on site [E0: industry assumption]
- Office manager (candidate): may maintain the team's lists and members in small offices instead of the lead [E0: industry assumption]

## Critical assumptions
The statements most product decisions depend on. Status changes only through a decision.
- Team leads plan and hand out the team's work in one weekly session, and "what is due today and whose is it" is the view they and the team need most [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: today view, assignment, due dates, and the default list layout all depend on it | Status: open
- Teams adopt a separate list next to their chat only if joining takes seconds with no account, email, or password; account requirements are a real reason other tools fail [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: the whole name-plus-sign-in-key identity model and the 2-minute invite success criterion rest on it | Status: open
- Team leads can introduce a free tool for their team without asking the owner [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: go-to-market (who to address), whether an owner/approver view is needed, and pricing | Status: open
- One named person per task is enough; no multiple assignees, roles, or teams inside a list [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: data model, assignment UI, and the "what's mine" view | Status: open
- Team leads need to see who completed what and when, so they stop chasing status in chat [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: completion attribution, activity history, and notifications to the lead | Status: open
- Chat remains the main communication channel; the list must be shared into chat by link and not try to replace chat [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: invite-by-link flow, whether comments/threads are built, and notification strategy | Status: open
- Freelancers or temporary helpers join team lists regularly and must be removable when the project ends [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: member management, revoking sign-in keys, list ownership | Status: open
- Flat lists without projects, subtasks, or dependencies are sufficient for the team's weekly work [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: confirms the "no full project management" non-goal and limits scope | Status: open
- When a member loses access (new phone, lost sign-in key), the lead re-invites them and no self-service recovery is needed [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: identity model without email, support load, admin features | Status: open
- The lead plans on a desktop while the team executes on phones [E0: industry assumption] | Critical because: responsive design priorities, bulk entry vs. quick check-off | Status: open
