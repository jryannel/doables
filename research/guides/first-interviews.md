# Guide: First discovery interviews

As of: 2026-09-26 | Based on: personas/team-lead.md, personas/household-organizer.md, personas/task-contributor.md (all Version 1, all statements E0/E1)

Ground rule: ask about the past and concrete behavior, not opinions about the future or feature wishes. Record problems and solution ideas separately. Don't show or describe Doables before the test questions are done.

After the interview: put the raw notes into `private/` and run `/new-interview private/<file>` (pseudonymizes and creates `interviews/discovery/INT-xxx.md`), then `/analyze-interview`.

---

## Role: Team lead
Screen for: plans or hands out work for a team of 2–20 in an agency, freelancer collective, office, or trades business.

### Discovery questions
1. "Tell me about the last time you planned your team's week, from start to finish."
2. "What was the most time-consuming or annoying part?"
3. "What have you already tried to improve it?"
4. "Who else is involved? Who has to approve, who receives the result?"
5. "What are you measured on in your role?"

### Test questions for critical assumptions
1. Weekly planning / today view: "Walk me through last week: when did you decide who does what, and how often did you change it afterwards? What did you look at first thing each morning?"
2. No-account joining: "Think of the last tool you tried to bring into the team. How did people get in, and did anyone not make it in? What happened then?"
3. Tool choice without owner: "The last time you started using a new tool for the team, who did you have to ask, if anyone? What would have happened if it had cost money?"
4. One person per task: "In the last two weeks, was there a task that more than one person was responsible for? How did you handle it?"
5. Who completed what: "The last time you needed to know whether something was done, how did you find out? How long did that take?"
6. Chat stays central: "Where did the last three tasks you handed out actually get handed out: chat, meeting, tool, in person? And where did people reply?"
7. Freelancers join and leave: "When did an external person or freelancer last work with your team? How did they get access to what they needed, and what happened to that access afterwards?"
8. Flat lists suffice: "Show me or describe how you wrote down the last bigger job. Did you split it into sub-steps or depend on other tasks? Where did you keep that?"
9. Lost access: "The last time someone on your team lost access to a tool (new phone, forgotten password), what happened and who fixed it?"
10. Desktop vs. phone: "Where were you and what device did you use when you last planned the week? And where were your team members when they checked their tasks?"

## Role: Household organizer
Screen for: the person who organizes chores, shopping, or events in a household, flat share, club, or association.

### Discovery questions
1. "Tell me about the last time you organized chores, shopping, or helpers for an event, from start to finish."
2. "What was the most time-consuming or annoying part?"
3. "What have you already tried to improve it?"
4. "Who else is involved? Who has to agree, who just needs to know?"
5. "How do you know things are going well? What makes you feel it's working?"

### Test questions for critical assumptions
1. Reminding vs. listing: "Tell me about the last time something on the plan didn't get done. What did you do, and how did that feel?"
2. No-account joining and name-only trust: "The last time you asked the group to use an app or shared list, who joined, who didn't, and why? Did anyone ask who can see or change things?"
3. Recurring tasks: "Which tasks come back every week in your household or club? How do you keep track of whose turn it is right now?"
4. Acceptance without authority: "When you last set up a plan for the group, how did the others react? Did anyone change it or ignore it?"
5. Clubs like households (ask club coordinators only): "When you last needed helpers, how did you get people to take tasks? Could you tell anyone to do something, or did you have to ask for volunteers?"
6. Fairness counts: "The last time there was a discussion about who does more, how did it start and how was it settled? Did anyone point to numbers or a record?"
7. Mixed devices: "What phones and devices does everyone in the group use? Has that ever stopped you from using a particular app?"
8. Shopping list as entry point: "Walk me through the last shopping trip: how did you know what to buy, and who added things to that list?"
9. No email / privacy: "When the group last picked an app or chat group, did anyone raise concerns about phone numbers, email addresses, or privacy? What happened?"
10. Willingness to pay: "Have you or the group ever paid for an app or tool to organize things together? What was it, and who paid?"

## Role: Task contributor
Screen for: someone who regularly receives tasks from a team lead, flatmate, family member, or club organizer and does not organize the list themselves.

### Discovery questions
1. "Tell me about the last time someone gave you a task to do, from how you heard about it to when it was done."
2. "What was the most time-consuming or annoying part?"
3. "What have you already tried to keep track of tasks from others?"
4. "Who else is involved? Who gives you tasks, who checks whether they're done?"
5. "What do people expect from you in this group, and how do you know you're doing well?"

### Test questions for critical assumptions
1. No account/install: "The last time someone sent you an invite to a shared list or app, what did you have to do to get in? Did you finish, and if not, where did you stop?"
2. What's mine today: "The last time you opened a shared list, what were you looking for? What did you do right after?"
3. Mark done in list vs. chat: "Think of the last task you finished for someone else. How did they find out it was done?"
4. Return trigger: "What made you open that list or app last time: a notification, a message, a habit, or something else?"
5. Visible name: "In the tools you use with this group, can others see what you did? How do you feel about that at work compared to at home?"
6. Keeping access: "The last time you got a new phone or used a different computer, how did you get back into your shared lists? Did you lose anything?"
7. Adding tasks themselves: "When did you last add something to a shared list rather than just ticking it off? What was it?"
8. Several lists and contexts: "How many shared lists or groups do you use for tasks right now, across work, home, and clubs? How do you keep them apart?"
9. English UI: "Which apps do you use in English rather than German? Has a language ever made you give up on an app?"
10. Phone only on site (ask trades workers only): "Describe your last day on a job site: when did you look at your phone for work, and what was the connection like?"
